How to change vehicle colour in RC in Kerala

Last updated: October 2026

Yes, you can change your vehicle colour in Kerala, but only in the right order: get approval from the registering authority first, then repaint or wrap the vehicle, then get the new colour entered in the Registration Certificate (RC). A colour change not recorded in the RC leaves a mismatch between the vehicle on the road and the vehicle on record, and that mismatch causes trouble at traffic checks, at resale, and with insurance claims.

What Kerala changed in October 2026

Kerala's Motor Vehicles Department (MVD) has permitted seven types of vehicle modifications, subject to prior approval and compliance with the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 and the applicable rules. A report published on 7 October 2026, describing an order issued by Transport Commissioner G. Sparjan Kumar, lists change of colour first among the seven. The same report stresses that prior approval is essential: owners cannot carry out the modification first and seek approval afterwards, and all approved changes must be recorded in the RC. I have not read the order text itself, so treat this list as reported, and confirm the position with your RTO before you spend money on paint.

The seven categories named in that report are: change of colour, engine replacement with an identical unit from the same manufacturer, chassis-frame replacement with a new frame of the same type, body-structure modification, change of fuel type using an approved retrofitment kit, propulsion-system modification, and conversion into a disability-friendly vehicle. The order does not legalise unrestricted modification: alterations outside the permitted categories remain prohibited unless specifically authorised.

The legal rule behind the process

Section 52 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 restricts an owner from altering a vehicle so that the particulars in the RC differ from those originally specified by the manufacturer, except where the Act, the prescribed conditions, or an applicable approval permits it. Colour is recorded in the RC, so a repaint is an alteration for this purpose even though it does not change how the vehicle runs. Section 52(3) also sets a reporting rule: where an alteration has been made without approval, the owner must report it within 14 days, forward the RC, and pay the prescribed fee. Do not rely on that 14-day route as a plan. The Kerala order requires approval before the work.

Colours you cannot choose

Rule 121 of the Central Motor Vehicle Rules is cited in guides for one absolute ban: no motor vehicle may be painted olive green except vehicles belonging to the Defence Department. Do not pick olive green or a military green shade for a civilian vehicle. Guides also warn against reflective or chrome finishes and shades that imitate emergency vehicles. If you are unsure whether a shade is permitted, ask the RTO before repainting, not after.

Documents to keep ready

  • Original RC
  • Valid motor insurance certificate
  • Valid Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate
  • Owner identity and address proof, such as Aadhaar
  • Workshop invoice showing the new colour and the work done, kept for the inspection
  • Financier consent, if the vehicle is under a loan or hire-purchase agreement, since an alteration on a financed vehicle needs the registered owner's or financier's consent under the Act
  • Any approval letter or acknowledgement the RTO gives at the prior-approval stage

One form-number warning: secondary guides disagree here. Some name Form 33, others name a Notice for Alteration in Motor Vehicle (NAMV). Form 33 is the form used for change of address and other RC particulars, so do not assume a single form number from a guide. On Vahan, look for the service named Alteration of Motor Vehicle, and follow the prompts shown for your own registration number. A spelling error in the owner's name is a different service, covered in our name correction in RC guide, and a move to a new address is covered in our change of address in RC guide.

Steps: approval first, paint second

  1. Check the shade first. Confirm with your RTO that the colour you want is permitted.
  2. Apply for prior approval. On Vahan, open Vehicle Related Services, select Kerala and your RTO, enter the registration number, authenticate as the registered owner, and choose Alteration of Motor Vehicle. Enter the proposed colour, upload the documents asked for, and pay the fee shown.
  3. Wait for approval. Do not book the repaint until approval is in hand. Alteration is generally a non-contactless service, so you may be asked to visit with the vehicle and original documents.
  4. Get the work done and keep the invoice. Repaint or wrap the vehicle in the approved shade and keep the workshop invoice for inspection.
  5. Present the vehicle for inspection. Take the vehicle, RC, approval, and invoice to the inspection point named in your application.
  6. Check the updated RC. Confirm the new colour appears in the RC record and in the digital RC, using our RC download guide if needed.
  7. Tell your insurer the same week. Send the insurer the updated RC and ask for the policy record to be amended.

What goes wrong when the RC is not updated

Three problems come up repeatedly in guides. First, a traffic check compares the vehicle with the RC on record, and a mismatch invites a fine or further checking. The exact penalty for a colour mismatch could not be verified on an official Kerala circular for this post, so no fine figure is quoted here. Second, a buyer checking a used vehicle will find the mismatch in the RC record, which complicates resale and can delay a transfer. Third, an insurer deciding a theft or accident claim works from the policy record, which follows the RC: an unreported colour gives the insurer a reason to dispute the claim.

FAQs

Is changing my car colour legal in Kerala?

Yes, with prior approval from the registering authority and with the new colour entered in the RC. The October 2026 Kerala MVD order reported above lists colour change among seven permitted modifications, subject to approval. Without approval and an updated RC, the change is not complete in law.

Can I repaint first and update the RC later?

Section 52(3) gives a 14-day reporting rule for an alteration made without approval, but the Kerala order requires approval before the work. Repainting first puts you in the weaker position and risks an inspection problem if the shade is not permitted. Get approval first.

Do I need to change my insurance after a colour change?

Yes. Tell your insurer as soon as the RC is updated and ask for the policy record to be amended to the new colour. Keep the insurer's written confirmation with the updated RC.

What if my vehicle is financed?

Get the financier's consent before the alteration. A vehicle under a hire-purchase agreement cannot be altered without the registered owner's consent under the Act, and the RTO may ask for that consent document during the application.
