What documents to carry while driving in Kerala

Last updated: October 2026

For a private vehicle in Kerala, carry four documents: your driving licence, the registration certificate (RC), a valid insurance certificate, and a valid Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate. That is the direct answer. You may show them as originals or as electronic records pulled in DigiLocker or the mParivahan app, which the central government treats as at par with the originals. A photo or screenshot saved in your phone gallery is not the same thing, and this post explains why.

The checklist: what each document proves

DocumentWhat it provesWho carries it
Driving licence (DL)You are licensed for that vehicle classThe driver, always their own licence
Registration certificate (RC)The vehicle is registered and who owns itIn the vehicle, whoever is driving
Insurance certificateThird-party cover is in forceIn the vehicle, with the RC
PUC certificateThe vehicle passed its emission testIn the vehicle, with the RC

The legal duty comes from Section 130 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. On demand by a police officer in uniform, the driver must produce the licence for examination, and the owner (or the driver in the owner's absence) must produce the RC and the insurance certificate. For a transport vehicle, the fitness certificate and the permit must also be produced. Learner licence holders carry the learner's licence instead of a full licence, and must still display L plates and be accompanied as the rules require.

Digital copies: what counts and what does not

In an advisory reported by the Press Information Bureau on 9 August 2018, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways told states to accept the driving licence, RC and other documents in electronic form presented through DigiLocker or the mParivahan platform as valid under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, treating them at par with certificates issued by transport authorities. The advisory adds that these electronic records are legally recognised under the Information Technology Act, 2000. Our earlier guides also cite Kerala Police Circular No. 25 of 2018 to the same effect for Kerala checks.

What counts is the record pulled live inside DigiLocker or mParivahan, fetched from the Vahan or Sarathi database. What does not count is a scanned copy, a photograph of the card, or a screenshot stored in your gallery, because an officer cannot verify those against the database. The same 2018 advisory says that if the vehicle details on mParivahan or the eChallan app already show an insurance policy in force, the physical insurance certificate is not to be insisted on. If a document does not appear in either app, carry the paper original until the record is fixed. Our guides to downloading your driving licence and downloading your RC from DigiLocker and mParivahan walk through getting those records in place before you travel.

Extra documents for transport and commercial vehicles

If you drive a taxi, auto-rickshaw, bus or goods vehicle, the four-document list is not enough. Add these:

  • Certificate of fitness, in force for the vehicle
  • The permit for the route or service the vehicle is operating under
  • A conductor's licence, where the vehicle carries a conductor

For tourist vehicles, see our tourist permit guide. For fitness testing itself, see the automated testing station guide. A private car needs no fitness certificate and no permit, so do not let anyone sell you one for a private vehicle.

Driving someone else's vehicle

The documents split two ways. The licence is personal: you carry your own, valid for the class you are driving. The RC, insurance and PUC belong to the vehicle, so they must be in the vehicle no matter who drives it. Before borrowing a car or bike, check three expiry dates: the insurance end date, the PUC validity, and your own licence validity. If the owner cannot show you a valid insurance certificate, do not drive the vehicle: an accident in an uninsured vehicle leaves the owner and the driver exposed, and the fine for driving without insurance applies to the vehicle on the road.

What happens if a document is missing at a check

There are two very different situations, and only one has a grace period:

  1. The document exists and is in force, but is not with you. Section 130(3) of the Act says that where a certificate or permit is not in the person's possession at the time of demand, the person must submit copies within fifteen days from the date of demand to the officer who demanded it. Keep the demand note or challan and meet that date exactly. The dates on your own challan govern your case.
  2. The document has expired or never existed. There is no grace period for this. An expired insurance policy or PUC, or driving without ever holding a licence, is an offence in itself, and producing the document later does not undo it.

At the checkpoint, stay polite, show what you have, and ask what exactly is recorded as missing. If a challan is issued, check it before leaving: vehicle number, section, and amount. Our e-challan payment guide covers paying it, and our challan dispute guide covers the route if a detail on it is wrong.

Before every trip: a two-minute check

  1. Open DigiLocker or mParivahan and confirm the DL and RC records load. If either is missing there, carry the paper original.
  2. Check the insurance end date and the PUC validity date. Renew whichever ends first before a long trip.
  3. Confirm the licence class matches the vehicle: a two-wheeler licence does not cover a car, and a private licence does not cover a transport vehicle.
  4. If you are driving someone else's vehicle, confirm the RC, insurance and PUC are in the vehicle before you start.

FAQs

Is a photo of my RC on my phone enough?

No. Only the record pulled inside DigiLocker or mParivahan counts as an electronic original. A gallery photo or screenshot cannot be verified against the Vahan database.

Do I still need paper copies if I have DigiLocker?

Not for documents that appear correctly in DigiLocker or mParivahan. Carry paper for anything that does not appear there, and for documents the app does not carry.

What if my phone battery dies during a check?

Then you cannot show the digital record, which is why keeping the paper originals in the vehicle as backup is still the safest habit, especially on long trips.

Does a learner licence count as a driving licence at a check?

Yes, for the class it covers, while it is valid. You must still follow learner conditions, including L plates and an accompanying licence holder.

I bought a used vehicle and the RC still shows the old owner. What do I carry?

Carry the RC as it stands plus your transfer application acknowledgement, and finish the transfer promptly: see our RC transfer guide. Until the transfer is recorded, the RC on record is the old owner's.

What if the officer says a digital copy is not accepted?

Show the record inside the app, not a screenshot, and note the officer's name, the place and the time. The 2018 central advisory and the Kerala Police circular cited above both support acceptance of app-pulled records.
