How to check AI camera fine in Kerala

Last updated: October 2026

To check an AI camera fine in Kerala, open the Parivahan e-challan service at echallan.parivahan.gov.in, search by your vehicle registration number, challan number, or driving licence number, complete the captcha, and click Get Detail. You will see every pending and paid challan on the vehicle, including the offence, date, location, photo evidence, and the amount to pay. The Kerala Police payment service offers the same search by vehicle number, and challans that have moved to court appear on the Virtual Courts portal. Camera fines carry the same official amounts as any other challan: Rs.500 each for helmet and seat belt offences, Rs.2,000 for handheld phone use, and Rs.1,500 for a first speeding offence on a light motor vehicle.

What the Safe Kerala AI cameras check

The Safe Kerala project installed 726 AI cameras across the state. At launch, 675 of them were configured to detect helmet and seat belt violations and only four for speeding, with 692 set to impose fines when enforcement began. The cameras automatically flag riding without a helmet, driving without a seat belt, triple riding, handheld phone use, red-light and signal violations, and no-parking offences.

Two practical consequences follow. First, the system issues separate challans for each person: if the driver and the front passenger are both unbelted, the camera issues two challans, because the seat belt fine applies per person. Second, notices can arrive late. The camera network faced service interruptions in 2026 when internet service to the cameras lapsed over unpaid service bills; after it was restored, notices were expected for violations recorded during the gap. Central rules require an electronic notice of offence to be sent within fifteen days of the offence, so compare the offence date on your challan with the day you received it. Camera challans are sent to the mobile number registered against the vehicle, so keep that number current on Parivahan.

AI camera fine amounts in Kerala

Kerala compounds several Motor Vehicles Act fines below the Act's own penalty, including the helmet and seat belt fines, which the state's cabinet cut from Rs.1,000 to Rs.500. These amounts were checked against a current Kerala compounding schedule on 30 September 2026. The amount printed on your challan is final, so confirm it before paying:

Method 1: check on Parivahan

  1. Open the e-challan service. Go to echallan.parivahan.gov.in and open the Check Challan Status or payment service shown on the page.
  2. Search. Enter your vehicle registration number, challan number, or driving licence number. The portal may ask for an OTP sent to the mobile number linked to the vehicle.
  3. Complete the captcha. Enter the security code shown and click Get Detail.
  4. Review each challan. Check the offence, section, date, location, the photo evidence, and the amount. Camera challans carry a photograph, so open it.
  5. Pay if the challan is pending. Pay by card, net banking, or UPI, and download the acknowledgement. Save the receipt.

Method 2: check on the Kerala Police payment service

  1. Open the payment service. Go to payment.keralapolice.gov.in.
  2. Enter your vehicle registration number. Use the format the portal asks for, such as KL-01-AA-1234.
  3. Submit the search. The system shows cases and challans connected with the registration number, with options to view paid and unpaid cases.
  4. Select the fines to pay. Verify the offence, date, location, and amount for each before continuing to the payment gateway.
  5. Save the invoice. The transaction status appears after payment, and you can generate the invoice for your records.

Method 3: check on the Virtual Courts portal

Challans that move to court appear on the Virtual Courts portal, where Kerala Police directs users to check and pay them:

  1. Open the Virtual Courts portal. Go to vcourts.gov.in.
  2. Select Kerala and the traffic department option shown. The exact menu can change, so follow the live portal.
  3. Search. Use the supported identifier: mobile number, challan number, party name, or vehicle number.
  4. Verify the case details. Check the case number, offence, and the proposed fine carefully.
  5. Pay if online payment is permitted. Complete the OTP verification, accept the terms, pay by the available method, and save the receipt. If the portal does not permit online disposal, follow the court directions shown for that case.

How long do you have to pay an AI camera fine?

An unpaid Kerala challan is commonly reported to stay payable on the portal for 60 days from the date of issue. If it is not cleared in that period, it can be forwarded to a virtual court for further action. A newer central procedure (Central Motor Vehicles Rule 167, rewritten with effect from 21 January 2026) sets a different clock: 45 days to pay or contest the challan on the portal with evidence, then 30 days to pay once it is accepted. I could not find a Kerala notification putting this timeline into effect, so treat the dates printed on your own challan as the authority and do not let the window lapse.

Unpaid challans can also attract licence consequences. Kerala's Transport Commissionerate issued Circular 18/2026 in June 2026, grading licence suspension and correctional training by offence; as reported, helmet offences under Sections 194C and 194D bring at least a three-month suspension and a one-day session. In October 2026 the Supreme Court tightened recovery further, with unpaid challans able to block RC, PUC, and driving licence renewal services. If the challan is wrong

Camera challans live or die by their photograph. Open it and check whether the plate was misread, whether the vehicle is actually yours, and whether the offence happened as described. If the challan is wrong, use the Complaint tab on the Parivahan e-challan portal: enter the required details, describe the issue, attach documents or the image, and track the ticket number that is sent to your mobile. Contest within the window printed on the challan.

Phishing warning: do not pay from a link that arrived by SMS or a messaging app. Traffic-challan phishing is common. Open echallan.parivahan.gov.in or payment.keralapolice.gov.in yourself and verify the challan before entering card, UPI, or banking details.

FAQs

How do I know a fine came from an AI camera and not a police officer?

The challan shows the offence, date, time, and location, and camera-issued challans carry a photograph of the violation.

I never got an SMS. Can I still be fined?

Yes. Challans go to the mobile number registered against the vehicle, which may be outdated, and notices have arrived late during the network's service interruptions. Check your registration number on Parivahan periodically; unawareness does not stop the payment window.

What if I sold the vehicle before the camera caught it?

Camera challans are raised against the registration number, not the rider, so they land on whoever is named on the RC record. Until the transfer is processed, that is the seller. This is why completing the ownership transfer within fourteen days of the sale matters; our RC transfer guide explains the seller's risk in detail.

Can an AI camera fine suspend my licence?

Under Circular 18/2026, as reported, helmet and pillion offences bring at least a three-month suspension plus a one-day training session, and speeding brings at least three months with three days of classes. Ask your RTO how the circular applies to your case.

How long does an AI camera fine stay valid?

The commonly reported window is 60 days from the date of issue on the portal, after which the challan can be forwarded to a virtual court. A challan that is not settled does not lapse or get written off; it is escalated. The exact deadline on your challan is the one that governs your case.
