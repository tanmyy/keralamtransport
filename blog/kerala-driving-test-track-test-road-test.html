Kerala driving test: the track test and road test, what examiners check, and what happens if you fail

Last updated: October 2026

The Kerala driving test costs Rs.300 per vehicle class as the official test fee, and you can appear only after holding a learner's licence for at least 30 days. The test has two parts: a ground test of manoeuvres, which under the Kerala MVD's 2024 rules includes real-life challenges like angular parking, parallel parking, zig-zag driving and gradient testing plus the H course, and then a road test on public roads with traffic. If you fail, your application stays active: you wait 7 days, rebook on Sarathi, and pay the test fee again. This guide covers the official fee, the current format, what examiners score for two-wheelers and cars, what to carry on test day, and what a fail means.

The official fee: Rs.300 per class

Who can appear for the test

  • Learner's licence held for at least 30 days for the class being tested.
  • A booked slot on Sarathi: the test is slot-only. See our slot booking guide.
  • LL still valid: the test must be taken inside the 6-month LL window. Our guide to when to apply for the permanent licence covers the timing.

The two parts of the Kerala test: ground test and road test

The traditional format is a ground test of control, the 8 course for two and three wheelers and the H course for four wheelers, followed by a road test. The Kerala MVD's circular of 4 April 2024 changed the ground portion: applicants now face real-life driving challenges, the road test runs on public roads with traffic, and the assessment includes angular parking, parallel parking, zig-zag driving and gradient testing, followed by the H test on the ground. Carandbike and Autonexa describe it this way; the circular text itself could not be read on an official site, so treat fine points as reported, not verified.

Kerala-specific rules from the 2024 changes

  • Two-wheeler: the test vehicle must be a motorcycle with manual gears and an engine capacity above 95 cc.
  • Four-wheeler: no electric or automatic cars are allowed for the driving test.
  • Driving school test vehicles: must carry a dashboard camera and location tracker; the test is recorded, submitted to the Motor Vehicle Inspector, and the memory card is kept for three months.
  • Driving school vehicles older than 15 years are not allowed for tests under the same rules.
  • Daily cap: only 30 driving tests per day, comprising 20 new applicants and 10 from the earlier failed batch.
  • Legal position: driving school associations challenged the rules in the Kerala High Court, which refused to stay them. The current enforcement position could not be confirmed on mvd.kerala.gov.in, so ask your RTO which format your test will follow.

What examiners check: four-wheeler

ManoeuvreWhat is scored
H courseClean steering through the course without touching boundary markers, no stalling on the turns
Angular and parallel parkingCorrect entry angle, use of mirrors before and during reversing, parking inside the bay without touching markers
Zig-zag drivingSteering control at low speed through the marked path
Gradient testControlled hill start with minimal rollback; national automated-track guides put the typical tolerance around 30 cm, which is a guide figure, not a Kerala official figure
Road test with trafficLane discipline, signalling every turn and lane change, visible mirror checks, correct speed for the stretch, and behaviour at junctions

The same themes run through examiner guidance used across RTOs: smooth throttle and clutch from the first metre, visible head movement toward the mirrors, a signal for every turn, and neither speeding nor crawling. A single stall is usually noted rather than failed outright; repeated stalling forms a pattern the examiner cannot ignore.

Remember the Kerala vehicle rule: arrive on a geared motorcycle above 95 cc. An applicant who shows up on a scooter or a smaller bike is turned away before the test starts. Real applicant experiences are collected in our companion post on what to expect at the Kerala driving test.

Documents and vehicle to bring

  • Original learner's licence for the class being tested.
  • Application acknowledgement printout, or clearly visible on the phone.
  • One original photo ID: Aadhaar, voter ID or passport.
  • Your own test vehicle, if the RTO expects you to bring one. Confirm with the RTO, since some centres provide vehicles.
  • If bringing your own vehicle: Registration Certificate, valid insurance, valid PUC certificate, and the L plate displayed, with a licensed driver accompanying you.
  • Transport or heavy vehicle applicants: training completion certificate, Form 1A medical certificate, and the Class 8 pass certificate.

A missing document means being turned away, per national RTO practice guides, so check the list the night before.

Steps: book the driving test on Sarathi

  1. Complete 30 days on the learner's licence. No test date can be booked before the wait is over.
  2. Open Sarathi, select Kerala, then choose the driving test option under driving licence services.
  3. Enter your application details, upload what the portal asks for, and pay the test fee. The schedule figure is Rs.300 per class; the payment screen shows the final total.
  4. Choose your RTO and a date. Tests run in batches under the daily cap, so book as soon as dates open.
  5. Arrive in your batch on time with the documents and vehicle, and wait for your queue number.

What happens if you fail

  • Your application stays active. A failed test is not a rejected application; only the test itself is rebooked.
  • Wait 7 days from the failure date before rebooking, per the consistent guide practice across states.
  • Rebook on Sarathi and pay the recalculated fee. No separate official retest figure could be verified, so the new checkout total is the figure to pay.
  • A missed slot is a forfeited fee. If you know you cannot attend, reschedule in advance on the portal.
  • Attempts limit: no official Kerala cap could be verified for this guide. Guides describe either limited attempts or unlimited attempts within the LL's validity, so go by the Sarathi screen and the RTO.
  • If the learner's licence expires first, apply for a fresh LL and restart the 30-day wait before testing again.
  • Book early after a fail: retest slots are the scarcer half of the 30-per-day cap.

FAQs

Can I take the Kerala driving test in an automatic car?

No. The 2024 MVD rules bar electric and automatic cars from the driving test. Take the test in a manual car.

Can I take the two-wheeler test on my scooter?

No. The test vehicle must be a motorcycle with manual gears above 95 cc. Practise on the bike you will be tested on.

Is the road test really on a public road now?

That is what the 2024 circular introduced: the road test on a public road with traffic plus the ground manoeuvres. Confirm with your RTO how your centre currently runs it.

Does failing the driving test affect my learner's licence?

No. The LL stays valid and only the test is rebooked after the 7-day wait, with a fresh fee.

How soon after failing can I get a new date?

Book the new slot after 7 days. Practical availability depends on your RTO's daily cap, so check the portal rather than counting on a fixed timeline.
