Vehicle fitness test at an Automated Testing Station in Kerala

Last updated: October 2026

A fitness test at an Automated Testing Station (ATS) is not a visual inspection by an officer. The vehicle is driven through a test lane where calibrated machines measure its brakes, suspension, alignment, speedometer, lights and emissions, and the system generates the test report automatically. For transport vehicles, fitness testing moved to this automated model from 1 October 2024, and in Kerala you book through the Vahan appointment portal for the station linked to your RTO.

Who has to go to an ATS, and how often

The ATS rules, notified as G.S.R. 652(E) on 23 September 2021 and summarised by the Press Information Bureau, set two different rhythms. Transport vehicles are tested every 2 years up to 8 years old, and every year once they are older than 8 years. Private vehicles are tested at renewal of registration, at 15 years, and then at the shorter intervals that apply to renewed registrations, commonly every 5 years. The 8-year line matters: a 9-year-old lorry is tested every year, a 7-year-old one every 2 years, each time with a fee and a day off the road.

Transport vehicle up to 8 years oldEvery 2 years

What an ATS actually checks

The test has three parts: visual checks, equipment-based checks, and an emission check. The visual part covers documents, insurance and identification, steering play, chassis and frame condition, CNG or LPG safety, fuel tank and piping, exhaust and catalytic converter, engine mountings, battery, seatbelts, tyres including the spare, lights and signals, oil leaks, springs and shock absorbers, windscreen, wipers, doors, horn, tool box, first aid kit, fire extinguisher, warning triangle and registration plates.

Guides on the ATS regime also list horn, exhaust noise, speed governor where fitted, headlight performance, vehicle weight and axle load among the measured items. The practical point is that every result is a number, not an opinion, so worn brakes, tired suspension, poor alignment and an engine out of tune are failures a workshop can predict before the test.

Documents to keep ready

  • Registration Certificate (RC) of the vehicle
  • Form 38, the application for renewal of the fitness certificate, where the portal asks for it
  • Valid insurance certificate
  • Valid PUC certificate, see our PUC certificate in Kerala guide
  • Previous fitness certificate, for a renewal
  • Valid permit, for a transport vehicle that holds one, see our Kerala tourist vehicle permit guide
  • Road tax payment receipts, current

Clear tax, insurance and challan arrears before booking: a vehicle with lapsed tax or insurance cannot be presented for testing until the record is cleared.

Steps: book and attend the ATS test in Kerala

  1. Check the expiry first. Read the fitness validity on the RC or on the Vahan vehicle details record, and book well ahead of expiry, since station capacity is finite and appointments are booked ahead.
  2. Open the official portal. Appointments for ATS fitness tests are made through the online portal developed by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, reached through parivahan.gov.in and Vahan vehicle services. Select Kerala and the RTO where the vehicle is registered.
  3. Start the fitness service. Enter the registration number, complete the chassis and OTP verification, and choose the ATS appointment option.
  4. Pick the station and slot. The portal shows the ATS options linked to your RTO. Kochi is the Kerala centre named in the central list, and Kerala's state transport undertaking invited partners in November 2024 to set up automated centres at six locations including Chathannoor in Kollam and Ponnani in Malappuram, so go by the stations the portal offers you today, not by a list in a guide.
  5. Pay the fee shown and save the acknowledgement. Pay only through the portal gateway, and keep the application number and receipt.
  6. Service the vehicle before the test. Brakes, suspension, alignment, lights, tyres and emissions are the measured failures, so a workshop check on those items is cheaper than a failed test plus a retest.
  7. Attend with the vehicle and the documents. Bring the RC, insurance, PUC, previous certificate and booking acknowledgement.
  8. Collect the report. The test report is generated automatically after all tests are complete and sent to the registered owner electronically. If the vehicle passes, the fitness certificate follows from the registering authority on that report.

If the vehicle fails

A failure is not the end of the road, but it is not a negotiation either. Under the ATS rules, the owner may opt for a retest after paying the retest fee. If the vehicle fails the retest as well, it is declared an End of Life Vehicle (ELV), and scrapping, not another repair, is the route: see our guide to vehicle scrapping in Kerala.

If you are not satisfied with a test result, the rules give a separate appeal route: appeal with the requisite fee, and the appellate authority may order a partial or complete re-inspection within 15 working days. If the vehicle passes that re-inspection, the authority orders the certificate to issue, and its decision is final. A retest is a fresh test after repairs; an appeal asks for the original result to be re-examined. Repair and retest is the normal route for a genuine defect.

While a transport vehicle has no valid fitness certificate, it cannot legally operate, so it stops earning until the defect is fixed and the retest is passed. Book the retest as soon as the repair is done.

Preparing so the first test passes

Fix the measured failures before the test: uneven braking or a weak parking brake, a vehicle that sits unevenly or pulls to one side, dim headlights or broken lamps, worn tyres, a missing spare, fire extinguisher or warning triangle, faulty seatbelts, and an engine that smokes or runs rich. Where a speed governor is fitted, confirm it works and the speedometer reads true. A workshop check on brakes, suspension, alignment, lights, tyres and emissions is cheaper than a failed test plus a retest.

FAQs

Is the ATS test mandatory for my private car in Kerala?

Not on the transport-vehicle cycle. A private car is tested at registration renewal after 15 years and then at renewed-registration intervals, and that test is increasingly instrumented in the same way.

Where is the ATS in Kerala?

Kochi is the Kerala location named in the central list of inspection and certification centres. Kerala has also tendered for further automated centres, including Chathannoor in Kollam and Ponnani in Malappuram. No live Kerala ATS directory could be verified on an official page for this post, since mvd.kerala.gov.in did not respond at research time, so confirm the station for your RTO on the Vahan appointment portal or at your RTO counter.

How much does the ATS fitness test cost?

See the fee table above: Rs.400 for a motorcycle, Rs.600 for a three-wheeler or LMV, and Rs.1,000 for a medium or heavy vehicle, plus Rs.200 for the certificate and Rs.50 per day for delay. The Vahan payment screen for your own application is final.

Can the ATS repair my vehicle if it fails?

No. The ATS rules make the station a test-only facility with no financial interest in repairs, vehicle sales or spares. Repairs happen at a workshop of your choice, then you return for a retest.

What happens if my vehicle fails twice?

Under the ATS rules, a vehicle that fails the retest is declared an End of Life Vehicle, and the scrapping route applies.

Can I appeal a failed result instead of repairing?

You can appeal with the requisite fee, and the appellate authority may order a re-inspection within 15 working days. If the vehicle passes that re-inspection, the certificate is ordered. An appeal does not extend the validity of an expired certificate while it is decided.

Fees and rules change without notice. Verify all fees and rules on parivahan.gov.in before applying, and treat the fee total and the station shown on your own Vahan application as the final figure.
