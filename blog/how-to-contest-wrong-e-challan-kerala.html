How to contest a wrong e-challan in Kerala

Last updated: October 2026

If a Kerala e-challan is wrong, do not pay it just to close it. Check the photo evidence on the official portal first, then contest it in the same window you would use to pay: file a grievance on the Parivahan e-challan portal while the case is still there, or choose "I wish to contest the case" on the Virtual Courts portal if it has already been forwarded to a virtual court. Contest before you pay: guides describe payment as acceptance of the challan.

First, check whether the challan is actually wrong

Open the challan record on echallan.parivahan.gov.in yourself, by typing the address into your browser, and read four things: the vehicle number, the date and time, the place, and the photo evidence. Many "wrong challan" cases turn out to be a similar-looking vehicle, a vehicle sold years ago, or a duplicate of a challan already paid. The table below covers the common error types and what proves each one.

One warning: check the challan is real. Fake challan messages with payment links circulate regularly, and official messages do not ask for card details inside the text. If the message matches no entry on the official portal, it is not a challan you owe.

The clock: contest inside the same window as payment

Under the amended Rule 167 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, in force from 21 January 2026, you have 45 days from the date a challan is issued to pay it or contest it on the portal. If you do neither, the challan is treated as accepted and you get 30 more days to pay. Our guide to how long to pay a Kerala challan explains that timeline in full, including why older Kerala guides still quote about 60 days: that was the earlier portal practice, and some challan records still show it. Work to the shortest window, and treat the dates on your own challan as final. Ignoring a challan does not cancel it; it moves to a virtual court, where the court decides what you pay.

Under the same rule, the authority is expected to decide a contest within 30 days, and a challan it does not resolve in that period ceases to have effect. No Kerala notification adopting this timeline was found in this research, so follow the dates on your own challan record if they differ.

Step by step: contest on the Parivahan grievance portal

  1. Open the official portal yourself. Type echallan.parivahan.gov.in into your browser, not a link from a message.
  2. Find the challan first. Search by challan number, vehicle number, or driving licence number, and screenshot the photo evidence and full details.
  3. Open the Complaint tab. This opens the grievance system, also at echallan.parivahan.gov.in/gsticket/.
  4. Enter your details. Name, mobile number, and challan number are mandatory. Add your driving licence number if asked.
  5. Pick the error type. Choose the grievance type that matches your case, such as wrong vehicle number or wrong violation, and write a short, factual explanation of the error.
  6. Upload your evidence. Attach the RC, the challan photo, and whatever proves the error: toll receipts, a service invoice, a first payment receipt, or transfer papers.
  7. Verify with the OTP. Confirm with the one-time password sent to your registered mobile number.
  8. Note the complaint number. Save it, and track the complaint at the same grievance address.

Guides say the traffic authority is expected to resolve a grievance within 15 working days. That figure comes from procedure guides, not an official page read for this post, so keep checking your complaint status. If the portal asks for more information, answer promptly; guides warn that an unanswered query can close a complaint.

The Kerala route for AI camera challans

Kerala's AI camera network generates many disputed challans, usually over a misread plate or a similar-looking vehicle. For these, Kerala guides describe a second route alongside the Parivahan grievance: take the printed challan record, the photograph, and proof of where your vehicle actually was to the Regional Transport Office listed on the challan. Toll receipts, fuel bills, and service invoices are the evidence named most often, because they place the vehicle elsewhere at the time. Guides also name the Kerala Police Thuna portal as a complaint route for police-issued challans. If the case is already in virtual court, dispute it there, not at the RTO counter.

If the challan has already gone to Virtual Court

  1. Open vcourts.gov.in yourself and select Kerala and the traffic option shown.
  2. Search for the case by mobile number, challan number, party name, or vehicle number, as the portal offers.
  3. Read the case carefully. Check the offence and the proposed amount the court shows.
  4. Choose "I wish to contest the case." The case then goes for adjudication instead of closing by payment, and you can appear virtually for the hearing.
  5. File your evidence with the case. Attach the same material: the challan photo, your RC, and the documents proving the error.
  6. Keep the court record. Save every order. If the court upholds the challan, pay the amount it fixes; if it cancels it, keep the order with the vehicle papers.

Do not pay the same challan on both portals. Once a case is with the court, the court record is the one that counts.

If you already paid a wrong challan

No simple refund route could be verified for this post. Guides describe payment as acceptance of the challan, which is why contesting first matters. Some guides say a court can still be approached for a refund where the vehicle details were plainly wrong or there was a clear system error, but that is a discretionary court remedy, not a portal process, with no fixed refund timeline verified. If you paid by mistake, keep the receipt and transaction ID, file the grievance anyway with the receipt attached, and take legal advice if the amount justifies it.

FAQs

Is there a fee for contesting a challan?

No separate contest fee appears in the portal flows above. The figure in dispute is the fine printed on the challan. For offence amounts, see our Kerala traffic fine list 2026.

Can I contest a challan after I pay it?

Not through the normal portal route, because payment is treated as acceptance. Guides describe only a discretionary court application for a refund after payment.

What if the challan is for a vehicle I sold?

File the grievance with the sale and transfer papers, and make sure the RC transfer is actually completed, because challans follow the vehicle until the transfer is formally recorded. See our guide to RC transfer in Kerala.

How do I check an AI camera fine before deciding?

Open the challan record on Parivahan and look at the photo evidence, as in our guide to checking an AI camera fine in Kerala. A photo showing a different vehicle is your strongest ground.
