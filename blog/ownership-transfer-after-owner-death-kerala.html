Ownership transfer after owner death in Kerala

Last updated: October 2026

When a registered owner dies, the vehicle does not pass to the family automatically on paper. The person who succeeds to the vehicle, usually a spouse, son, daughter or the nominee recorded on the RC, must apply to the registering authority in Form 31 under Rule 56 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules. Parivahan gives two clocks: inform the registering authority of the death and of your intention to use the vehicle within 30 days, and file the Form 31 transfer application within 3 months of the death. The official transfer fee is Rs.150 for a two-wheeler and Rs.300 for a car (non-transport LMV), plus Rs.200 if a smart card RC is issued. All documents and fees below are from Parivahan, checked in October 2026.

The two deadlines that matter

DeadlineWhat you must do
Within 30 days of the deathInform the registering authority where the vehicle is registered that the owner has died and that you intend to use the vehicle. Parivahan states this condition for the interim use below.
Up to 3 months from the deathIf you have informed the authority within 30 days, you may use the vehicle for up to 3 months as if it had already been transferred to you, while the paperwork is completed.
Within 3 months of the deathApply in Form 31 for the transfer of ownership into your name and pay the fee and tax specified in Rule 81.

Do not read the 3-month interim use as permission to skip the transfer. Insurance, challans and any later sale all depend on the RC name. If the 3 months have already passed, apply anyway and follow the directions your RTO gives.

Documents Parivahan lists for a death transfer

  • Form 31, filled and signed (in duplicate if the vehicle is under hire-purchase, lease or hypothecation)
  • Original certificate of registration (RC)
  • Death certificate of the registered owner
  • Proof of succession
  • Declaration by the applicant and by all other legal heirs of the deceased
  • Certificate of insurance, valid
  • Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate, valid
  • PAN card of the successor, or Form 60 if there is no PAN
  • Proof of address and proof of date of birth of the successor
  • Pencil print of the chassis and engine number
  • Passport-size photograph of the successor
  • Signature identification of the successor

Parivahan marks several items with an asterisk, noting they may be required in some states. Prepare the full list anyway: a missing declaration from one legal heir can stop the file.

Legal heir certificate, succession certificate, or a will: which proof of succession?

Parivahan lists only the heading "proof of succession" and does not name one Kerala document for every case, so be careful with any page that promises one document always works. The proof has to show that you are an heir, and the declaration of the other legal heirs records their position on the transfer into your name. Where a court has decided succession, the court order governs. Where a nominee was recorded on the RC, the current Form 31 also covers the person nominated, but the death certificate and identity and address proofs are still needed. I could not verify a current Kerala MVD page stating case by case when a legal heir certificate is accepted and when a court succession certificate is insisted on, so confirm with your registering authority before paying for court paperwork you may not need.

Steps: how the transfer is filed in Kerala

  1. Note the dates first. Count 30 days and 3 months from the date of death on the death certificate. If the 30-day intimation window is still open, send that intimation to the registering authority first.
  2. Gather the heir documents. Get the death certificate, your proof of succession, and the declaration of the other legal heirs first. These take the longest.
  3. Clear the vehicle's record. Renew insurance or PUC if either has lapsed, and check for pending challans.
  4. Start on Vahan. Go to parivahan.gov.in, open Vehicle Related Services, select Kerala, enter the registration number, validate with the last 5 digits of the chassis number and the OTP, then choose Transfer of Ownership and the death of owner option.
  5. Fill Form 31 details and pay. Enter the successor details exactly as they appear on your ID and address proof, review the fee panel, and pay the official fee shown there.
  6. Upload or submit the documents. Upload the documents listed above where the portal asks for them, and keep the acknowledgement number the system generates.
  7. Attend the RTO if called. Be ready to produce the original RC, the death certificate and the succession papers in person at the registering authority.
  8. Collect the new RC and update insurance. Once the transfer is recorded, have the insurance policy transferred or reissued in the successor's name, so the RC and the policy match.

If the vehicle still has a loan

Form 31 has a financier consent section for vehicles held under hire-purchase, lease or hypothecation, and it is filed in duplicate in those cases. If the loan is fully repaid, get the financier's no-objection certificate and close the hypothecation entry; our guide to hypothecation removal with Form 35 covers that separate step. The death transfer does not remove the loan entry on its own.

How this differs from a normal sale transfer

A sale transfer uses Forms 29 and 30, signed by seller and buyer, within 14 days in the same state. A death transfer has no seller signature: Form 31, the death certificate and the succession proof replace it, and the window is 3 months. If the family later sells the vehicle, complete the death transfer into an heir's name first, then do a normal sale transfer. That second step is covered in our RC transfer fees and time guide.

FAQs

Can I drive the vehicle before the RC is transferred?

For up to 3 months from the death, yes, provided you informed the registering authority within 30 days of the death and of your intention to use the vehicle. After that, the vehicle should be in the successor's name.

What is the fee to transfer a car after the owner's death in Kerala?

The official transfer fee for a private car is Rs.300, which is half of the Rs.600 registration fee for a non-transport LMV in the Parivahan table, plus Rs.200 if a smart card RC is issued. A two-wheeler is Rs.150 plus the same smart card charge where it applies.

Is a succession certificate always compulsory?

Parivahan requires proof of succession but does not name one document for every case. An uncontested transfer among agreeing heirs is a different file from a disputed one, and a court order governs where a court has decided succession. Confirm the exact document your RTO wants before starting court paperwork.

What if there are several legal heirs?

The RC can carry only one registered owner. The other heirs give the declaration Parivahan lists, recording their position on the transfer into the applicant's name. If the heirs cannot agree, the RTO cannot settle that dispute for you; it has to be resolved first, through agreement or a court order.

Can the vehicle be sold directly from the deceased owner's name?

No. A dead person cannot sign Forms 29 and 30. Transfer the vehicle into an heir's name through Form 31 first, then sell it with a normal transfer.

I missed the 3-month window. What now?

Apply in Form 31 anyway; a late application is still the only route to a correct RC. Follow any direction your registering authority gives for the delay.
