Kerala driving licence renewal fees: the official table and late charges

Last updated: September 2026

The official Kerala driving licence renewal fee is Rs.200 under Rule 32 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, and a Kerala applicant's actual payment receipt shows Rs.400 in total on time for an LMV or motorcycle-with-gear licence: Rs.200 for the renewal plus Rs.200 for the smart card. You get a 30-day grace period after expiry during which you still pay the standard amount. Apply later than that and the rules add Rs.300 plus Rs.1,000 for every year (or part of a year) of delay, counted from the end of the grace period. Applicants aged 40 and above also need a Form 1A medical certificate signed by a registered doctor.

The official on-time fee table

Fee itemOfficial amount
Renewal of driving licence (Rule 32, item 8)Rs.200
Smart card issue (Form 7)Rs.200
Total paid on time in Kerala (applicant receipt, LMV and motorcycle with gear)Rs.400

The renewal and smart card amounts come from the central fee schedule. Some independent guides quote on-time totals between Rs.400 and Rs.600 because states can add small administrative or postal charges, and those figures conflict with each other across aggregator sites. The fee the Parivahan portal shows you at checkout is the final one; a driving school that files the application for you will quote more, so treat anything above this table as a service charge and ask for an itemised quote.

Late renewal: the grace period, the penalties and the five-year trap

Section 15 of the Motor Vehicles Act gives you a 30-day grace window after the expiry date, during which renewal is treated as a standard on-time renewal with no penalty. After that window, the late charges kick in.

When you applyAdditional chargeWhat happens
Within 30 days of expiryNoneStandard renewal, total around Rs.400
More than 30 days after expiryRs.300, plus Rs.1,000 per year (or part of a year) of delay from the end of the grace periodRenewal still processed, no driving test
Expired five years or moreThe same late charges, accumulatedYou may have to start fresh: a new learner's licence and both the theory and practical driving tests

A worked example: a licence that expired 2 years and 2 months ago attracts roughly Rs.400 (standard fee) plus Rs.300 (late application charge) plus Rs.3,000 (three part-years at Rs.1,000 each), which comes to about Rs.3,700.

Do not keep driving on an expired licence while you delay. Driving with an invalid licence is an offence under Section 181 of the MV Act and carries a Rs.5,000 fine, separate from any renewal charges. It can also complicate insurance claims.

Form 1A: the medical certificate rule for applicants aged 40 and above

In Kerala, the Parivahan portal asks applicants aged 40 and above to submit Form 1A, a medical fitness certificate, along with a certificate for visual standards for driving. This is confirmed by a Kerala applicant who renewed online in 2025: at age 40 he was asked for both, and he got them signed at an eye hospital in Palakkad before re-uploading. Some national guides describe the Form 1A rule as applying from age 50, but the Kerala practice verified on the portal is 40.

  • Form 1A must be completed, signed and stamped by a doctor registered with the Medical Council of India or a state medical council, holding at minimum an MBBS qualification. Any MBBS doctor in private practice can issue it.
  • The doctor typically checks vision (6/9 in each eye with or without glasses for a private licence), hearing, blood pressure and basic fitness, and the examination takes about 10 to 20 minutes.
  • The doctor's own consultation fee is a private charge, not part of the RTO fee. Reported fees vary between roughly Rs.200 and Rs.800 in most cities; confirm with the doctor before you go.
  • Form 1, the self-declaration of physical fitness, is a separate form and is required for every applicant regardless of age; it does not need a doctor.

Get the medical certificate reasonably close to your submission date, and make sure the doctor's seal and signature are clear on the scanned copy. Blurred uploads are a common reason applications get held for re-upload.

Steps: renew online in Kerala

  1. Go to sarathi.parivahan.gov.in (or parivahan.gov.in, then Online Services, then Driving Licence Related Services).
  2. Select Kerala as the state.
  3. Click Apply for DL Renewal.
  4. Enter your driving licence number and date of birth. The portal fetches your existing record.
  5. Fill Form 9: confirm your details and select the services you need (renewal, plus address change if required).
  6. Upload a recent passport-size photograph, your scanned signature, and your documents, including Form 1 and Form 1A if you are 40 or above.
  7. Pay the official fee online by UPI, debit or credit card, or net banking. Save the acknowledgement number.
  8. If the portal asks for it, book an RTO slot for biometric capture or photo update. A Kerala applicant completed the entire renewal online without any RTO visit, but the portal decides case by case, so follow what it shows you.
  9. Track the application under Application Status. The renewed smart card is posted to the address on record.

You can apply for renewal up to one year before the expiry date, which is the easiest way to avoid the late charges entirely.

Documents checklist

  • Existing driving licence: a clear photo of the front and back of the card. One applicant's renewal was held up because he uploaded a downloaded PDF instead of a card photo, so photograph the physical card.
  • Form 1: self-declaration of physical fitness (filled on the portal).
  • Form 1A medical certificate with the doctor's signature and seal: required at age 40 and above in Kerala.
  • Recent passport-size photograph and scanned signature, in the sizes the portal specifies.
  • Age and address proof: Aadhaar, passport, voter ID, or a utility bill.
  • Payment receipt from the portal.

Common mistakes that delay renewals

  • Uploading a downloaded or Digilocker PDF of the old licence instead of a photo of the physical card.
  • Renewing before updating a changed address: the smart card posts to the address on record. Fix the address first through the address change process, then renew.
  • Letting a lapse drift past 30 days, which adds Rs.1,000 per year, or past five years, which forces a fresh application.
  • Paying without reading the fee breakup on the checkout screen, especially when an intermediary files on your behalf.

FAQ

Do I need to take a driving test to renew?

No, a standard renewal has no driving test. The only exception is a licence that has been expired for five years or more, which the RTO may treat as a fresh application with a learner's licence and both tests.

Can I renew my licence before it expires?

Yes. Renewal applications can be filed up to one year before the expiry date, and there is no downside to doing it early.

Can I complete the renewal without visiting the RTO?

Often yes in Kerala: one applicant renewed fully online without a visit in 2025. The portal sometimes asks for a biometric or photo update at the RTO, in which case you book a slot and carry the originals.

What if my old licence is lost as well as expired?

You can apply for a duplicate licence alongside the renewal; the duplicate carries its own Rs.200 fee under the same fee schedule. See our duplicate driving licence guide for the combined process.

Why does the portal show a higher amount than Rs.400?

Check the fee breakup on screen. Late charges, address changes filed in the same application, or state-specific administrative charges can raise the total. What matters is the official receipt, not a figure quoted elsewhere online.
